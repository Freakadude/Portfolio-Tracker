# ADR 0025: Look-through aggregation and the concentration rule

Status: accepted (2026-10-05)

## Decisions
- **Open up, then add up.** Every valued position that has a holdings snapshot is replaced by its constituents, each worth the ETF's value times the constituent's weight; every other position stays itself. The pieces ("atoms") are then added up by company, sector, country or currency. This is pure code in `folio/analytics/lookthrough.py` (mypy strict, in the coverage gate), and the exposures always add up to the value of the positions that were looked at (a property test). A file whose weights add up to more than 100 % is scaled down rather than inventing value.
- **What the file does not cover stays visible.** Cash, derivatives and a top-N file leave a remainder, shown as "Other holdings of <ETF>", so a partial file never makes the portfolio look smaller or more concentrated than it is.
- **A fund without holdings is not a company.** It stays itself, is marked as a fund, and is never counted by the concentration rule ("World ETF is 40 % of your portfolio" is not a finding). The widgets list such funds so the owner knows which holdings files are missing. Likewise the unnamed rest of an ETF and the "Unclassified" group never trigger the rule.
- **Companies are matched by ISIN when a file has one, else by a normalised name** (lower case, no punctuation, no legal suffixes such as Inc, NV, SA, Holding), so "ASML Holding N.V." in an iShares file and "ASML HOLDING NV" in another are one company. Countries (a code, "USA", or the full name) and the most common sector spellings ("Technology" and "Information Technology") are mapped to one name before adding up. A direct share's country is its ISIN's country (stored as the instrument's domicile); a fund's is not guessed.
- **The result carries its breakdown.** Each exposure lists where it comes from (held directly, or inside which ETF, with the weight in that ETF), so one company held directly and in two ETFs shows one combined figure and an explanation, which is the FR-PF-05 acceptance.
- **One function feeds the API, the widgets, the simulator and the rules.** `look_through_at` serves `GET /portfolio/allocation?look_through=true`, `GET /portfolio/look-through`, the allocation and look-through widgets and the rules' inputs; the simulator opens its before and after positions the same way and shows the fifteen largest company changes. An ETF's own allocation grouping (sleeve, asset class, region) is unchanged; asking to look through with one of those groupings is refused with the reason.
- **`concentration_limit` now fires** on a company or a country whose share of the portfolio, counting holdings inside ETFs, is above the rule's `limit_pct` or, for companies, `risk_limits.max_single_company_lookthrough_pct`. Its message lists the places the exposure sits; the push text has no euro amounts and the anonymous text no names. Until a limit is set, or while no ETF has holdings, the rule says what it is waiting for.

## Consequences
Look-through is as good as the files: names that differ across issuers and have no ISIN can still be split into two exposures, and the owner sees both lines. The country and sector mappings are small on purpose and easy to extend.
