# ADR 0027: Clustering stories and linking them to holdings

Status: accepted (2026-10-05)

## Decisions
- **Clusters by headline words, within 48 hours.** Items are compared by the meaningful words of their headlines (lower case, no stop words). Two items are one story when they were published within 48 hours of each other and share at least half of their words (shared words as a share of all words of both: Jaccard), or at least 30 % when both are linked to the same holding, or when the headlines are word for word alike. There are no embeddings and no extra dependency: this is cheap, explainable and wrong in the cautious direction (two sources that phrase one story very differently stay two clusters, which costs a second line on the News page, never a hidden story). An item joins the cluster whose closest member it resembles most. A cluster keeps the headline of its earliest item and the span of its items.
- **Links come from the text, the ETFs and the strategy, in that order of trust.** For each item, deterministic matching finds the holdings and watched instruments (never an instrument that is neither) by: an ISIN in the text, a ticker EODHD tagged the article with, an upper-case ticker of three or more letters as a whole word (so "F" and "ON" never match), and the instrument's aliases as whole-word phrases. Every instrument gets one alias made from its own name ("ASML Holding N.V." gives "asml"); the owner can add more, and feedback lowers an alias's weight, which switches it off below 0.3 (FR-NW-08). Then the ETFs: the 150 largest constituents of each held ETF's newest holdings snapshot (ADR 0024) are matched by ISIN or name, and a hit is a `look_through` link to the ETF carrying the company's weight in it ("Apple, 5.2 % of the World ETF"). A company held directly and through an ETF links both ways. Names shorter than four letters are never used.
- **Macro stories link to sleeves.** A source that speaks to macro series (the ECB's deposit rate; the Fed's funds rate, real yield and dollar) links a story to the sleeves that watch one of those series, or that a `macro_threshold` rule on the series applies to, in the active and shadow strategies. Only stories that are about rates, money or the currency count (a short list of terms), so a Fed approval of a bank merger links to nothing. The link type is `macro` and it names the series (`macro:DFII10`).
- **Relevance, 0 to 1, from four things.** The way the link was found (direct 1, look-through 0.8, macro 0.5, theme 0.4), how much of the portfolio it touches (a floor of 0.2 so a watchlist item still counts, rising to full at 10 % of the portfolio; for look-through, the ETF's share times the company's weight in it), the source's trust, and freshness, which halves every 24 hours; an alias's weight multiplies it. A cluster's relevance is its best link's. These numbers are heuristics, kept in one place (`folio/news/linking.py`) and covered by tests, so they can be tuned.
- **Redone, not accumulated.** Each time a cluster gains an item its deterministic links are rebuilt from all its items, so a changed portfolio or a switched-off alias takes effect on the next story. Links made by the LLM (themes, wider macro links, step 6) carry `llm:` in `matched_by` and are never replaced here.
- **Unlinked stories are kept** (cheap, and a holding added tomorrow may make yesterday's story matter) but have relevance 0; the News page shows linked stories by default.

## Consequences
Matching by name can be wrong for common words used as company names; the four-letter minimum, the whole-word rule and per-alias feedback are the guards, and a wrongly linked story is one click from "not relevant". Sector and thematic links ("export rules on chip-making equipment") need the LLM and arrive with the impact assessment.
