# ADR 0026: News sources, polite fetching and what is stored

Status: accepted (2026-10-05)

## Decisions
- **An owner-edited list, with central banks ready-made (Q12).** Settings holds the list of sources (name, kind, address, language, trust 0.1 to 1, poll interval, on or off). Three come with the app: the ECB's and the Federal Reserve's press-release feeds, each tied to the macro series it speaks to (so a later step can link a Fed decision to the sleeve that watches the dollar or real yields), and EODHD's news for the held tickers, used only when an EODHD key exists. A default the owner deletes does not come back. Issuer feeds are added by the owner; the app ships none because their addresses change. Adding a feed shows its last ten items first (FR-NW-01), fetched with the same rules as the real thing and stored nowhere. The two default addresses were checked live on 2026-10-05 and the fixtures are recordings of them.
- **feedparser parses, the app downloads.** feedparser (pinned, with its small `sgmllib` helper) is the one new dependency; it reads RSS and Atom from bytes the app already fetched through its own HTTP client, so retries, the circuit breaker and call counting apply as for every provider, and feedparser never touches the network. An address that returns something that is no feed (feedparser leaves its `version` empty for HTML) is reported with advice instead of silently producing nothing.
- **Polite by construction (FR-NW-02).** Requests are conditional: the ETag and Last-Modified of the last answer are sent back, and a 304 is recorded without parsing anything. `robots.txt` is read once a day per site and obeyed (Python's parser, matching the name "Folio", else `*`); a site whose robots.txt cannot be read because it is failing (5xx) is left alone this time, one without a robots.txt (404) has nothing disallowed. Requests to one site are at least ten seconds apart (three for a preview in the web app). A failing source waits its own interval doubled for each failure in a row, at most a day, and shows its error in Settings and on the System page; the job's run is only marked failed from the third failure in a row, so one flaky feed does not make the System page cry wolf. The `news` job runs every 15 minutes from 07:00 to 23:00 Amsterdam time and hourly overnight, and each source also keeps to its own interval.
- **Headlines, short summaries and links only (FR-NW-03).** An item keeps its headline (300 characters at most), a plain-text summary (500 at most, cut at a word, markup and scripts removed), the link, the time in UTC, the source's language and a hash of the headline. There is no body column, and the article page is never fetched. EODHD returns article text in `content`; only its first 500 characters become the summary. The canonical link has no tracking parameters, fragment, `www.`, repeated slashes or trailing slash (the ECB's own feed writes `//press/...`), and is the identity of an item.
- **The language comes from the source setting.** No language-detection library: the sources are curated and the owner states the language; a wrongly stated one only affects the label.
- **EODHD news shares the daily call budget with the closes.** One news request counts as five calls (the call weight added with ADR 0024) and runs per tracked ticker, but only while the budget left after it still covers one call per tracked listing plus the margin of five for the nightly closes, as for delayed quotes (ADR 0017). The tickers EODHD tags an article with are kept on the item as hints for linking.
- **Fetch first, write after.** As elsewhere, calls counted against a budget use their own database connection, so the job commits before each fetch and writes only after it.

## Consequences
Two feeds are on by default, so Folio makes outbound requests to ecb.europa.eu and federalreserve.gov as soon as the worker runs; the README lists them (NFR-08). A site that blocks robots in its robots.txt cannot be added, by design.
